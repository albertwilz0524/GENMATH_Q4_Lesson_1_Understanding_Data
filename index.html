1. Types of Data

Qualitative (Categorical)

Describes qualities. Observed, not measured numerically.

Quantitative (Numerical)

Discrete: Countable (Whole numbers).

Continuous: Measurable (Decimals/Fractions).

2. Levels of Measurement (NOIR)

  • Nominal: Labels only. No order.
  • Ordinal: Meaningful order, but differences aren't uniform.
  • Interval: Uniform differences, NO true zero.
  • Ratio: True zero exists (0 means "none").

Practice Exercises

1. Zip Codes (e.g., 3000, 1001)

Nominal (It's just a label).

2. Academic Ranks (Instructor, Asst. Prof, Professor)

Ordinal (Ranked, but difference is vague).

3. Temperature in Celsius

Interval (0°C is not "no heat").

4. Daily Allowance (in Pesos)

Ratio (0 pesos means no money).

3. Measures of Central Tendency

A single value that attempts to describe a set of data by identifying the central position.

Measure Definition Best Used When...
Mean The average (Sum / Count). Data is numeric and has no outliers. (Interval/Ratio)
Median The middle value when sorted. Data has skewed distribution or outliers. (Ordinal/Interval/Ratio)
Mode The most frequent value. Data is categorical. (Nominal)

Measures of Variability

Describes how spread out or scattered the data values are.

  • Range: The difference between the highest and lowest value ($Max - Min$).
  • Variance ($s^2$): The average of the squared differences from the Mean.
  • Standard Deviation ($s$): The square root of the variance. It tells us, on average, how far each value is from the mean.

Note on Calculation: We usually deal with Sample Data in this course.

Use $n-1$ in the denominator for Variance.

Formulas (Sample Data)

Mean ($\bar{x}$): $$ \bar{x} = \frac{\sum x}{n} $$
Sample Variance ($s^2$): $$ s^2 = \frac{\sum (x - \bar{x})^2}{n - 1} $$
Sample Standard Deviation ($s$): $$ s = \sqrt{s^2} $$

Where $x$ is the value, $\bar{x}$ is the mean, and $n$ is the number of observations.

Example Calculation

Data Set: Quiz scores of 5 students: 12, 15, 13, 18, 12

Step 1: Calculate Mean

$$ \bar{x} = \frac{12 + 15 + 13 + 18 + 12}{5} = \frac{70}{5} = 14 $$

Step 2: Variance & Standard Deviation

Rule: Calculate Variance to 4 decimal places, then take square root for SD (2 decimal places).

$x$$x - \bar{x}$$(x - \bar{x})^2$
12$12 - 14 = -2$4
15$15 - 14 = 1$1
13$13 - 14 = -1$1
18$18 - 14 = 4$16
12$12 - 14 = -2$4
Sum26

Variance ($s^2$): $\frac{26}{5-1} = \frac{26}{4} = 6.5000$

Std Dev ($s$): $\sqrt{6.5000} \approx 2.5495... \rightarrow \mathbf{2.55}$

Try This!

Calculate the Mean, Median, Mode, Range, Variance ($s^2$), and Standard Deviation ($s$) for the following data set.

5, 12, 8, 5, 10

1. Central Tendency & Range

  • Sorted Data: 5, 5, 8, 10, 12
  • Mean ($\bar{x}$): $\frac{40}{5} = \mathbf{8}$
  • Median: Middle value = $\mathbf{8}$
  • Mode: Most frequent = $\mathbf{5}$
  • Range: $12 - 5 = \mathbf{7}$

2. Variability Table

$x$$x-\bar{x}$$(x-\bar{x})^2$
5$5-8 = -3$9
5$5-8 = -3$9
8$8-8 = 0$0
10$10-8 = 2$4
12$12-8 = 4$16
Sum38

3. Final Calculation

Variance ($s^2$): $\frac{38}{5-1} = \frac{38}{4} = \mathbf{9.5000}$

Standard Deviation ($s$): $\sqrt{9.5000} \approx 3.0822... \rightarrow \mathbf{3.08}$

Interpretation

Using the example from earlier (Mean=14, SD=2.55):

  • Mean = 14: The average score of the students is 14.
  • Standard Deviation = 2.55: On average, the students' scores deviate from the mean by approximately 2.55 points.

Why does this matter?

  • A low standard deviation means data is clustered closely around the mean (consistent).
  • A high standard deviation means data is spread out (volatile/inconsistent).

Using Technology

In the modern world, we use tools to calculate these values efficiently.

Scientific Calculator

Look for STAT mode.

  1. Enter Mode: STAT $\to$ 1-VAR
  2. Input Data ($X$ values)
  3. Press AC
  4. Shift $\to$ 1 (Stat) $\to$ Var
  5. Select $\bar{x}$ for Mean, $sx$ for Sample SD.

Microsoft Excel / Google Sheets

Formulas:

  • Mean: =AVERAGE(A1:A5)
  • Median: =MEDIAN(A1:A5)
  • Sample Variance: =VAR.S(A1:A5)
  • Sample Std Dev: =STDEV.S(A1:A5)
